JukeBar for iOS

JukeBar turns an iPhone or iPad into the whole jukebox: it plays your Apple Music playlist, runs the QR-code request system, and serves the admin/bartender screens — all from one device, no other hardware required. This page is the full manual: first-run setup, network options, troubleshooting, and the Apple Music-specific privacy notes. For the general pitch and pricing model, see the overview page.

Good fit for

Bars that already run their music through Apple Music (subscription streaming, or your own purchased/uploaded tracks) and want the whole setup to live on one iPhone or iPad, including offline nights when there's no house WiFi at all.

Setup guide

JukeBar walks through three screens on first launch (and again any time you use End Session from the admin panel — nothing here is a one-time, unchangeable choice).

1. Pick a kiosk mode

The very first screen asks how the kiosk should work:

  • Local Only — requests are entered directly on this device. No customer phones, no QR code, no network needed at all.
  • Remote Only — customers connect from their own phones after scanning the QR code shown on the kiosk.
  • Local + Remote — both at once: some customers can request right on the kiosk, others scan the QR code from their own phone.

2. Pick how customers connect (skipped in Local Only mode)

If you chose Remote Only or Local + Remote, the next screen picks the network type:

  • Bar WiFi — customers join the same router you're already on.
  • iPhone Hotspot — your iPhone becomes the router (enable first under Settings → Personal Hotspot); customers join your phone's hotspot directly, no external router involved.
  • Internet / Relay — customers connect over the internet from anywhere, via a QR link that works off any network. The iPhone still plays the music locally; only requests/approvals/now-playing pass through the relay.

3. The main setup wizard

  • Access Apple Music

    Grant JukeBar permission to read your Apple Music library and play songs.

  • Pick a playlist

    Choose the Apple Music playlist to use as the jukebox source.

  • Name your jukebox & discovery

    Give this JukeBar instance a name (useful if you run more than one venue/room). Optionally toggle "List on JukeBar map" — this publishes the bar name, an address you type in for the map marker, and the band names from your playlists to the JukeBar discovery map. Off by default, changeable any time via End Session.

  • Set the admin PIN

    4–6 digits, entered twice to confirm. Leave blank on a re-run to keep the existing PIN.

  • Choose payment methods

    Two independent toggles — 💳 Stripe (Pay Online) and 💵 Pay to bartender — enable one, both, or neither. Leave both off and every request is free and auto-approved, no bartender action needed. Stripe requires an internet connection, so it's unavailable in Local Only mode.

  • Set prices

    Per-song price and a discounted 3-song bundle price, plus your Stripe key if Stripe is enabled. Skip entirely if both payment toggles are off.

Admin controls

Once running, long-press the bar name on the kiosk screen and enter your PIN to reach admin controls: playback, catalog refresh, approval-mode toggle, and End Session — which is also how you change kiosk mode, network mode, playlist, or any other setup-wizard choice later.

Downloading songs

JukeBar plays songs that are downloaded to the device (or streams them if the device has internet and an active Apple Music subscription). In the Apple Music app, open your playlist, tap the download button (⬇), and confirm before starting a session — a cloud icon means a track isn't downloaded yet.

Bartender pairing

Share the bartender URL shown in admin settings with your bartender. They open it in any browser on the same network, enter their name and the bar PIN, and are ready to approve requests. The first person to pair becomes session admin; additional bartenders need admin approval.

Network setup

The network mode you pick during setup is exactly the three options from the wizard above — this section goes deeper on each.

Bar WiFi

Connect the iPhone and all customer/bartender devices to the same router.

Common issue — AP isolation

Most venue routers have client isolation ("AP Isolation") on by default on guest networks — it blocks devices from reaching each other even though they can all reach the internet. Fix: disable "Isolate WiFi clients" in the router's guest-network settings and reboot the router — many routers need a restart for the change to take effect.

iPhone Hotspot

Enable Personal Hotspot in iOS Settings first. Customers and bartenders join the iPhone's hotspot directly — no external router, no isolation issues. Requires a cellular plan that supports hotspot.

Tip

The most reliable option if you don't control the venue's router settings — the iPhone handles everything itself.

Internet / Relay

JukeBar connects to the jukebars.com relay over the internet. Customers use a shareable QR link that works on any network; bartenders can approve from anywhere with internet access. The iPhone still plays the music locally — only requests, approvals, and now-playing state pass through the relay, no song audio or personal data included.

FAQ — iOS & Apple Music specific

For network, PIN, reports, and other questions common to both platforms, see the general FAQ on the overview page.

Songs aren't playing / "playlist not found" error

Make sure every song in your Apple Music playlist is downloaded — songs with a cloud icon aren't. Open the Music app, find your playlist, and tap the download button.

If you recently added songs, use Refresh Catalog in admin settings to reload the playlist.

Can I change kiosk mode or network mode after setup?

Yes — open admin controls (long-press bar name → PIN) and choose End Session. This runs the setup wizard again from the kiosk-mode screen, with everything pre-filled from your last session.

Does the community map use my exact GPS location?

JukeBar captures the device's approximate location once, at the point you enable "List on JukeBar map," to place your marker — separately, you also type in a display address shown on the marker itself. Both are only sent if you opt in; disabling the toggle stops future updates.

Privacy — Apple Music

See the full privacy table on the overview page for the complete picture — this section covers the one iOS-specific detail.

Playback uses Apple's MediaPlayer framework and your existing Apple Music subscription. JukeBar only reads your library to build the on-device catalog — it does not access your listening history, purchase history, or Apple ID account information, and none of that ever leaves the device. Apple's own privacy policy governs how Apple Music itself handles your data at the OS level.
